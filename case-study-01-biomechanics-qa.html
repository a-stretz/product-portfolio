Executive Summary

Built the platform’s first AI model performance evaluation system, giving the team a repeatable way to verify biomechanical metrics, heuristic scoring, keypoint stability, and generated feedback before those outputs reached users.

01

Context & Challenge

An edge AI startup had built a computer vision platform that generated biomechanical analysis from pose estimation data. The platform could produce outputs from 3D body landmark tracking, but no one had a formal way to prove whether those outputs were accurate, reliable, or safe to present as trusted coaching feedback.

There was no QA layer between raw model outputs and user-facing interpretation. Metric calculations had not been validated against ground truth. Measurement precision had not been quantified. The team also had not systematically tested whether certain categories of measurements were feasible given the limitations of pose estimation under occlusion and motion.

The core problem was not model generation. It was the absence of a systematic evaluation and QA framework for AI outputs.
02

My Role

I was the Product Lead responsible for the complete QA and validation system. I designed the evaluation architecture, built the multi-stage validation pipeline, established the ground truth methodology, conducted jitter analysis, and interpreted the findings into release-readiness decisions.

Implementation used AI-assisted development workflows with Claude Code and ChatGPT to accelerate iteration and test variations. The system design, validation logic, interpretation of results, and product decisions remained my responsibility.

03

Approach

I treated QA as a core product system, not a downstream check. The pipeline was designed to answer a specific release question: can we trust this system’s outputs enough to put them in front of users?

Phase 1

Data infrastructure

Converted serialized model outputs into structured tabular data, extracting landmark coordinates across tracked body points per frame with automatic subject identification.

Phase 2

Ground truth validation

Built a three-way comparison across model outputs, manual biomechanics annotation, and an independent automated calculation pathway.

Phase 3

Precision analysis

Built a nine-module jitter analysis suite to quantify keypoint instability, reference-frame wobble, and propagated angle error.

Phase 4

Production system

Scaled the prototype into a production metrics system with dual reference frames, batch processing, and v2.0 gravity-aligned updates.

04

Results

Validation produced a three-tier reliability pattern.

Jitter analysis quantified measurement precision.

ConditionPrecisionKeypoint Stability
Best case±2–3°<1.5 px jitter
Typical±4–6°2–5 px jitter
Worst case±10–15°5–12 px jitter
Critical finding: lower body landmarks showed materially higher instability during occlusion and motion. This was not noise that could simply be filtered out. It was a practical limitation of the underlying technology and directly shaped which metrics could ship.
05

Key Design Decisions

Decision

QA as product infrastructure

The pipeline evaluated model versions, metric changes, and pose-estimation upgrades against established ground truth rather than acting as a one-time audit.

Decision

Separate measurement from interpretation

The system separated statistical validation of numeric measurements from LLM-as-a-Judge review of generated coaching feedback.

Decision

Three-way validation

Model, manual, and independent automated calculation paths made it easier to distinguish model error from annotation error.

Decision

Surface limits aggressively

The goal was not to prove the system worked everywhere. The goal was to identify where it could be trusted and where it could not.
