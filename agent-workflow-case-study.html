Overview

Product ownership at NeoSavant.ai spans two AI product lines and nine productized systems, coordinated with roughly 40 internal engineers and 7 external partners across computer vision, ML, infrastructure, and field operations. Every one of those systems generates a steady stream of changes that need acceptance criteria, and until this build, each one was written by hand.

The fix wasn't a better template. It was an agent layer: project-specific agents that read the raw inputs a PM already produces, meeting transcripts and Slack threads, and turn them into a structured first draft.

01 · Problem

The bottleneck wasn't judgment. It was transcription.

Writing acceptance criteria doesn't require new information most of the time. The decisions were usually already made, in a meeting, in a Slack thread, in a back-and-forth with an engineer. The work was re-reading all of that and manually converting it into structured epics, user stories, and Definition of Done, one system at a time, across a backlog spanning nine products.

As the only product lead across that surface area, the iteration cycle for a single set of acceptance criteria could take a full day, not because the decision was hard, but because turning a conversation into a document was slow, repetitive, and easy to defer.

02 · Approach

Three stages, one operating loop.

Input

Meetings & Slack, as they already exist

No separate intake step. The agent reads the same meeting transcripts and Slack threads a PM already has, rather than requiring requirements to be re-entered into a structured form first.

Processing

Project-specific agents

Each of the nine productized systems gets its own agent context, so a draft is grounded in that system's specific requirements and history, not a generic prompt applied everywhere.

Output

A first-pass draft, not a final answer

The agent produces a structured starting point, epics, stories, acceptance criteria, for the PM to review and edit, rather than a decision that ships unreviewed.

The win wasn't "AI wrote my job for me." It was narrower and more honest than that: the specific, repetitive step of turning a conversation into a document got up to 10× faster. Everything upstream of that, the judgment calls, still belonged to the PM.
03 · Key Decisions

Four decisions that kept it useful.

Decision 01

Feed It What Already Exists

Used raw meeting transcripts and Slack threads as input, rather than building a separate structured-intake step that would itself become new manual work.

Decision 02

Scope Agents Per Project

One agent context per productized system, not one general-purpose prompt, so drafts stay grounded in that system's actual requirements rather than generic boilerplate.

Decision 03

Keep the PM in the Loop

Agents draft; the PM reviews, edits, and approves before anything reaches the backlog, consistent with the human-in-the-loop principle applied across every AI system built at NeoSavant.

Decision 04

Measure the Bottleneck It Actually Fixes

The 10× figure is scoped narrowly and deliberately to acceptance-criteria iteration time, not a claim about the entire product role.

04 · Skills Demonstrated

What the build exercises.

Capability 01

Building project-specific agents around existing inputs rather than requiring new process overhead.

Capability 02

Capability 03

◆   Final Summary

The PM function, automating its own busywork.

Meeting transcripts and Slack threads already contained the decisions. The agents just closed the gap between a conversation and a structured backlog item, cutting a day-long manual cycle down to minutes.

This project demonstrates practical AI agent design, requirements engineering, and a disciplined, narrowly-scoped approach to measuring AI-driven productivity gains.
