Overview

I designed and built an AI-assisted career workflow system that moves a candidate from job discovery to application strategy, tailored materials, interview preparation, and answer improvement.

The system is built around a structured candidate knowledge base rather than one-off prompt generation. The core product insight was simple. High-quality career outputs depend on reusable candidate context, not a fresh prompt every time. A strong story, proof point, positioning angle, or metric should not need to be recreated separately for every application, cover letter, recruiter message, resume version, or interview answer.

The product supports candidate intake and knowledge-base creation, job fit analysis, application decisioning, tailored artifact generation, interview question generation, answer evaluation, rewrite coaching, session review, and workflow history.

01 · Problem

Job search work is fragmented, and generative AI has made it worse.

Candidates manage job descriptions, resumes, cover letters, recruiter messages, company research, interview notes, and story banks across disconnected tools. The result is repeated work and inconsistent positioning across every opportunity pursued.

Generative AI tools can produce drafts, but they lack durable memory of the candidate's experience. They produce polished language, but outputs drift toward repetitive, generic, or weakly grounded claims, exactly the kind of content hiring systems are now trained to filter out. Most tools also work from whatever the candidate happens to paste in, usually a resume or a LinkedIn profile, which captures a fraction of the information that could actually make the case for a candidate while staying accurate and true to their real experience.

The deeper problem was never document creation. It was friction. The friction of putting your best case forward as a candidate, on the roles most likely to want you, in a way that doesn't just land the interview but sets up how to prepare for and win it. It's making the most of a job search by maximizing time, effort, and cost. Every part of that reduces to one question. How do you show a specific employer that you are their ideal candidate? The candidate needed a system that could answer that question end to end, not a set of isolated prompts.

The questions the system had to answer
  • Is this role worth pursuing?
  • What parts of my background are most relevant?
  • Which stories and proof points should I use?
  • How should I tailor my positioning for this company or role?
  • What application materials should I generate?
  • What interview questions should I prepare for?
  • How strong are my answers, and how should I improve them?
  • What have I already generated or practiced for this opportunity?

The product needed to create continuity across the entire workflow, not just solve one piece of it.

The system was designed for any professional with objective skill sets, experience with specific products, tools, processes, software, or languages. Because it assists users with diverse backgrounds, it also needed strict guardrails around accuracy. Every output has to emphasize real experience that demonstrates real capability, without under or overstating it.

03 · Product Design

One shared foundation, the candidate knowledge engine.

The product is organized around one shared foundation. Every workflow reads from and writes into the same structured knowledge layer, so context compounds instead of getting recreated.

Knowledge Engine

Stories as reusable product assets.

A story from the candidate's background can support a resume bullet, a cover letter paragraph, an interview answer, a recruiter message, a hiring manager note, or a portfolio summary.

Each story includes context, ownership, actions, outcomes, metrics, tags, strengths, risks, and interview relevance. The richer story vault adds interview questions, STAR structure, debrief notes, and weighting for technical depth, product impact, and transferability.

The system recommends not just "a story" but the right story for the situation.

04 · Core Workflows

Seven connected workflows on one knowledge layer.

Workflow 01 · Candidate Intake

Candidate Intake and Knowledge Base

The first step in the workflow, and the one everything else depends on. A structured intake process captures the candidate's full body of work, the complete extent of experience, tech stack, tools, software, achievements, domain knowledge, and industries, then organizes it into a structured knowledge base.

This is what makes downstream personalization accurate instead of generic, and what makes the workflow a repeatable, productized system rather than something built for one person.

Experience historyTech stack & tools Achievements & metricsDomain knowledge IndustriesSkills & fluency Writing examplesPositioning goals
Output A confirmed candidate knowledge base, reviewed and signed off by the user before any job processing begins.
Workflow 02 · Job Intake

Job Intake and Normalization

Once the candidate knowledge base is confirmed, the system is ready to process opportunities. It accepts pasted job descriptions, company notes, recruiter context, role labels, source links, or batch imports, and normalizes each into a structured job record.

CompanyRole title LocationRemote / hybrid / onsite CompensationSeniority Required skillsPreferred skills Product domainDisqualifiers
Output Clean job record ready for fit analysis, no more re-parsing raw job text on every run.
Workflow 03 · Fit & Decision

Job Fit Analysis and Decisioning

Evaluates the job record against the candidate knowledge base to determine whether an opportunity is worth pursuing. Deterministic checks handle location, compensation, and hard requirements, so those results stay transparent and testable. Model reasoning, meaning the language model's generated judgment, handles nuanced fit, role summarization, and positioning.

  • Responsibility alignment, domain relevance, AI or platform relevance
  • Technical skills match, requirements fit, compensation fit
  • Location, seniority, priority signals, reject criteria
Output Apply / reject / research decision, fit score, priority level, rationale, risk flags, recommended positioning angle, recommended next artifacts.
Workflow 04 · Artifacts

Tailored Application Materials

Generates application artifacts from the job record and the candidate knowledge base, replacing variant-based resume recommendation with tailored resume generation from a single controlled source of truth.

  • Selects relevant approved bullets
  • Adjusts summary for the target role
  • Emphasizes the right skills and projects
  • Avoids unsupported claims, preserves ownership boundaries
  • Explains what was changed and why
  • Drafts custom outreach messaging tailored to its recipient, such as a short LinkedIn connection request, an InMail, or an introduction email
  • Answers custom questions required by an employer's application process
Artifact types Tailored resume versions, cover letters, recruiter follow-ups, hiring manager notes, custom recipient messaging, fit summaries, thank-you notes, evaluation notes, tracker updates.
Workflow 05 · Interview Prep

Interview Preparation

Uses the same job context and knowledge base to run background research and a fit evaluation on the role and company, then generates role-specific practice sessions. The user configures style, difficulty, focus area, session length, weakness to test, answer length, and input mode (typed or voice).

Questions are designed to test specificity, decision-making, tradeoffs, product judgment, technical fluency, and business framing. The system also suggests which stories from the knowledge base are most relevant to each question.

Output Focused question set, grounded in both the target role and real background, with recommended stories per question.
Workflow 06 · Answer Coaching

Answer Evaluation and Coaching

A customized interview engine records the candidate's answers to expected questions and evaluates them against the specific job's criteria, adjusted by user-configured settings that shape how strict or lenient the evaluation runs. Feedback comes in two stages. Editorial markup breaks the answer into spans and labels each section, keep, cut, compress, reorder, upgrade, or pivot.

Rubric scoring evaluates across relevance, structure, specificity, product judgment, technical fluency, business framing, metrics and outcomes, conciseness, and credibility. The system identifies recurring weaknesses, such as rambling, missing metrics, weak structure, generic language, no clear result, or missing decision logic, and offers rewrite variants (core, full, detailed, more executive, more metrics-driven, more technically accessible).

Loop Answer → review → score → rewrite → retry → compare improvement.
Workflow 07 · History & Memory

History and Workflow Memory

Tracks analyzed jobs, generated artifacts, interview sessions, answer evaluations, recurring weaknesses, and session summaries. The user can return to a role and see what was already analyzed, generated, practiced, and improved, with progress tracking across jobs analyzed, roles pursued, applications generated, interviews prepared, recurring answer weaknesses, strongest stories, overused stories, readiness by role type, and improvement over time.

Better AI outputs don't come from a better prompt. They come from a system that already knows who you are.
05 · Key Product Decisions

Six decisions that shape how the system behaves.

Decision 01

Knowledge Base First

The strongest design decision. The system does not start from a blank prompt each time. It reuses structured context, so positioning, stories, and metrics compound across workflows.

Decision 02

Human-in-the-Loop Control

The system supports review, editing, overrides, story selection, retries, and rewrite choices. It assists the user without hiding the reasoning behind decisions.

Decision 03

Structured Scoring Over Generic Feedback

The interview system uses dimension-based scoring rather than vague coaching. This makes feedback actionable, comparable over time, and amenable to trend analysis.

Decision 04

Story Selection as a Product Feature

Answer quality depends heavily on story choice. The system helps the candidate match the right story to the right role, question, or artifact — not just produce "an" answer.

Decision 05

Deterministic Rules + Model Reasoning

Hard checks stay deterministic, so they stay transparent and testable. Model reasoning, meaning outputs generated by the underlying language model rather than fixed logic, handles nuance, drafting, evaluation, story matching, and summarization where judgment is required. The system runs on the Anthropic Claude API, so every model call is a live API request rather than a locally hosted model.

Decision 06

Artifact Memory

Generated outputs are saved, versioned, and tied to the job or session that produced them, in a structured database rather than kept in a model's context window. This keeps the system auditable and enables progress tracking across the full job search, without paying ongoing token or cache cost just to remember past work.

◆   Final Summary

An AI-assisted career workflow system that helps job seekers represent themselves the best way they can, in a challenging job market.

Starting from a structured candidate knowledge base, the system evaluates opportunities, recommends pursuit decisions, generates tailored materials, prepares role-specific interview questions, scores answers, identifies recurring weaknesses, and improves responses through rewrite coaching.

This project demonstrates product strategy, workflow design, knowledge architecture, AI system design, prompt orchestration, structured evaluation, and practical AI-enabled product execution.
