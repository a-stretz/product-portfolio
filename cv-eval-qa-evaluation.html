Why It Mattered

If a final score looked wrong, an end-to-end test alone couldn't tell us why. The wrong athlete could have been tracked, an action could have started late, a phase transition could have been late, a landmark could have been unstable, the measurement formula could have been wrong, or scoring configuration could have distorted a correct measurement.

The core principle: do not evaluate the whole system when you can isolate the variable that may be wrong. That required layered evaluation — a portfolio of complementary methods, not one QA pass.

01

My Role

I was the Product Lead responsible for the QA and validation strategy — designing the evaluation architecture, establishing ground-truth methodology, running precision/jitter analysis, and interpreting findings into release-readiness decisions. Implementation used AI-assisted development workflows to accelerate iteration; the system design, validation logic, and interpretation of results remained my responsibility.

02

System Context

The product pipeline runs from video through detection/tracking, pose, action boundary, phase boundary, metric window, measurement, score, to feedback. A structured output is more useful for QA than watching a playback and saying something "looks late" — so validation methods targeted specific stages of that chain.

03

A Portfolio of QA Methods

Six complementary methods, not one QA tool:

Method 01

Annotation & reference boundaries

Human-defined action/phase boundaries as a reference for comparison against model output. Ambiguity had to be documented, not treated as unquestioned truth.

Method 02

Model output review

Structured action/phase ranges with explicit frame numbers, enabling deterministic comparison of predicted vs. reference timing.

Method 03

Controlled frame-range validation

Known reference frames fed into the metric engine, bypassing production detection timing to isolate whether the metric behaves correctly given a correct frame.

Method 04

Expected vs. Actual review

Comparing the intended metric definition with what the implementation actually calculated — catching the class of failure where code correctly calculates the wrong thing.

Method 05

End-to-end pipeline evaluation

Reviewing model versions through the full inference pipeline, in the same environment where downstream behavior occurred — necessary but not sufficient on its own.

Method 06

AI-assisted validation experiments

Independent measurement paths that didn't depend on the same production coordinates and formulas being tested, including experiments with Claude and ChatGPT.

04

A Validation Method Must Itself Be Validated

One documented AI-assisted experiment attempted to independently measure angles from rendered pose keypoints visible in frames — using a visually independent path as a form of ground truth rather than asking the same coordinate system to validate itself. The implementation included keypoint-marker detection, configurable angle calculation, batch processing, and structured output.

It ultimately failed the reliability requirement: small visual markers, dynamic pose changes, and ambiguous keypoint identity made the approach too sensitive to image conditions to serve as trustworthy ground truth. I stopped investing in that methodology rather than accepting low-confidence QA because the tool happened to produce numbers.

That failed experiment was useful. It clarified an important evaluation principle: a validation method must itself be validated. At least one additional AI-assisted QA effort was run beyond this one; the surviving documentation doesn't support describing its implementation in detail, so it isn't reconstructed here.

Critical finding: lower-body landmarks showed materially higher instability during occlusion and motion — a practical limitation of the underlying technology, not noise that could simply be filtered out. That finding directly shaped which metrics could ship and at what confidence level.
06

Validation

This work contributed to the broader model-improvement initiative — QA served as the mechanism for determining whether interventions in training data, temporal logic, and measurement actually improved behavior, rather than assuming they had.

07

Outcome

QA turned a complex AI pipeline from a largely end-to-end black box into a set of testable interfaces.

  • Model / phase QA, heuristic tuning, and controlled comparison became routine, not one-off
  • Metric-timing validation and semantic-formula-defect detection ran independently of detector correctness
  • An unreliable validation method was identified and abandoned rather than kept for its numbers
  • Handoffs between Product, ML, Data Science, and engineering became clearer because failures could be isolated by layer
08

Limits & Attribution

I designed the QA architecture, ground-truth methodology, and precision analysis, and made the release-readiness calls that followed from them. AI-assisted workflows (Claude Code, ChatGPT) accelerated implementation and iteration, but the validation logic and the decisions built on it were mine. I did not train the underlying pose-estimation or action-detection models.
